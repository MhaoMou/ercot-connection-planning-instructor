\section{Assignment overview}

Each student group represents a data-center operator such as a cloud-computing
company. Your group submits a connection-and-resource plan. Submissions of
all groups will be collected and evaluated together using a DC optimal power
flow (\code{DCOPF}) dispatch model. \textbf{Groups are ranked by score.}

\subsection{What you must do}
Your team must design and submit one complete half-year connection plan in the
released CSV format. The goal is to serve valuable computational load while
controlling energy, interconnection, generation, and storage costs.

\subsection{Deliverables}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item one \code{UTF-8 CSV} file for your team;
  \item validator output showing that the file passes the selected \code{horizon};
  \item a short strategy note explaining your main bus, load, generator, and
  storage choices and the trade-offs you considered;
  \item the exact final CSV and any code used to generate it.
\end{enumerate}
The instructor will announce the file name, submission location, deadline,
round assignment, and whether the strategy note is required for grading. Do
not submit a screenshot in place of the CSV.

\subsection{Assignment rules at a glance}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Submit exactly one complete \code{half-year horizon}. The released calendar determines
  the length: 2016 has 4,368/4,416 hours and 2017--2018 have 4,344/4,416 hours
  for January--June/July--December.
  \item Use \code{bus IDs} and \code{generator IDs} from the supplied ACTIVSg2000 scenario block.
  \item Load is nonnegative and is specified directly in \code{MW} for every hour.
  \item \code{Generators} and \code{storage} are private to the submitting team.
  \item The current release has no student-controlled \code{curtailment} decision.
  \item The instructor evaluates valid teams jointly and ranks teams by \code{score}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Use of AI.} You are explicitly encouraged to use available AI tools while doing the
assignment, including ChatGPT, Codex, Claude Code, GitHub Copilot, and similar
tools. You remain responsible for checking generated code, understanding your
submission, respecting the CSV contract, and explaining your engineering
decisions. AI assistance is part of the intended computational workflow, not a
substitute for validation.

\subsection{Learning objectives}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item translate a planning idea into a machine-readable decision file;
  \item reason about spatial congestion, generator dispatch, and storage;
  \item understand why individually feasible plans can be jointly infeasible;
  \item use reproducible computational experiments and inspect diagnostics;
  \item communicate trade-offs among IT value, energy cost, and investment.
\end{itemize}

\section{Data and model: what is real and what is instructional}

The dispatch input is the public synthetic Texas ACTIVSg2000 model~\cite{activsg2000}, which
contains exactly \textbf{2,000 buses}, 3,206 branches, and 544 generator
records. The fixed topology is combined with a controlled three-year hourly
scenario package. The scenario provides deterministic bus-level load; active
generator status, PMIN, and PMAX values come directly from the dataset. Because
ACTIVSg2000 does not provide technology labels or hourly availability data, the
active release does not apply synthetic wind/solar deratings. Each block is
validated hour by hour with DCOPF.
It is an instructional scenario, not historical ERCOT telemetry or an official
ERCOT operating model.

The conceptual pipeline is:
\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.82\textwidth}{\centering
Public ACTIVSg2000 topology + controlled hourly operating profiles\\
$\Downarrow$\\
Fixed ACTIVSg2000 buses, branches, generators, costs, and scenario demand\\
$\Downarrow$\\
DCOPF input and teaching scoring scenario\\
$\Downarrow$\\
Joint evaluation of student CSV files
}}
\end{center}

The following distinction is important:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Public data:} \code{bus}, \code{branch}, \code{generator}, and
  marginal-cost records from the ACTIVSg2000 source model.
  \item \textbf{Instructor preparation:} 
  \code{formatting}, \code{validation}, and the full-horizon economic profiles supplied with the round.
  \item \textbf{Instructional assumptions:} the student-owned investments,
  connection requests, \code{DCOPF} treatment, \code{IT value}, capital-cost coefficients,
  storage-cost coefficients, and the \code{fair continuous projection} policy.
\end{itemize}

The exercise should not be interpreted as an ERCOT planning recommendation or
as a forecast of actual interconnection feasibility.

\section{Operating horizon and decisions}

Each submission governs \textbf{one half-year with a one-hour time step:}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
Year & Round & Hours & Zero-based period indices\\
\midrule
2016 & January--June & 4,368 & $0,\ldots,4367$\\
2016 & July--December & 4,416 & $0,\ldots,4415$\\
2017 or 2018 & January--June & 4,344 & $0,\ldots,4343$\\
2017 or 2018 & July--December & 4,416 & $0,\ldots,4415$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The source year contains one full calendar year's hourly records. A submission therefore has
access to roughly 2,000 possible buses and roughly 4,400 hourly decision slots,
but it does \emph{not} need to contain 2,000$\times$4,500 CSV rows. The long-form
CSV lists only the buses used by your team. For every bus that appears in the
file, however, the release evaluator requires one row for every hour in the
selected half-year; use a zero load in a row when that bus is idle. Buses that
never participate may be omitted entirely.

For each selected \code{bus} $s$ and hour $t$, you may request load $d_{s,t}\ge 0$.
You may also submit one or more private \code{generators} and one or more private
\code{storage devices} attached to your selected connection sites. A team's resources
serve only that team. They cannot export energy to another team or become a
shared pool.

\section{Mathematical model}

\subsection{Connection load}

Let $d_{i,s,t} \geq 0$ be the load requested by team $i$ at bus $s$ in hour
$t$. The corresponding connection capacity is
\[
  C_{i,s}=\max_t d_{i,s,t},\qquad
  p_{i,s,t}=\begin{cases}d_{i,s,t}/C_{i,s},&C_{i,s}>0,\\0,&C_{i,s}=0.\end{cases}
\]
The CSV stores the hourly load directly; the evaluator reconstructs the
capacity and normalized profile internally.

\subsection{Private generation}

\code{Generator IDs} refer to records in the ACTIVSg2000 generator catalog. The evaluator
uses the catalog's \code{marginal\_cost\_c1\_per\_mwh} value for the generator's
operating cost. The generator is physically attached to your chosen bus for the
exercise, but its cost reference comes from the public dataset. For a generator
with capacity $G_{i,g}$, dispatch satisfies
\[
  0\le g_{i,g,t}\le G_{i,g}.
\]
The instructor may impose additional catalog or site limits in the round
configuration.

For the current classroom release, the dispatch evaluator applies the
generator status, PMIN, and PMAX values from the ACTIVSg2000 catalog in every
hour. No synthetic technology-specific availability limit is applied.

\subsection{Private storage}

For storage device $b$, submit power capacity $P_{i,b}$ in MW and energy
capacity $B_{i,b}$ in MWh. Charging $c_{i,b,t}$ and discharging $q_{i,b,t}$ are
continuous. We assume ideal charging/discharging efficiency:
\[
  e_{i,b,t}=e_{i,b,t-1}+c_{i,b,t}-q_{i,b,t},
\]
with
\[
  0\le e_{i,b,t}\le B_{i,b},\qquad
  0\le c_{i,b,t},q_{i,b,t}\le P_{i,b},
\]
\[
  q_{i,b,t}\le e_{i,b,t-1},\qquad
  e_{i,b,0}=0.5B_{i,b},\qquad
  e_{i,b,H}\ge 0.5B_{i,b}.
\]
Storage is private to the submitting team. Charging appears as additional
team demand and discharging reduces that team's grid purchase; it does not
serve any other team's load.

\paragraph{Storage cost calibration.} The released half-year scoring files use
the following instructional coefficients:
\begin{align*}
 C^{\mathrm{storage,cap}}_{i,b}={}&
 (\USD200{,}000/\MW)P_{i,b}
 +(\USD300{,}000/\MWh)B_{i,b},\\
 C^{\mathrm{storage,op}}_{i,b}={}&
 (\USD8/\MWh)\times\text{discharged energy}.
\end{align*}
For a four-hour device, the two capital terms correspond to approximately
\USD1,400/kW of installed power capacity. These are rounded teaching
coefficients informed by U.S. battery-storage construction and O\&M benchmarks,
not a project quotation or an ERCOT tariff~\cite{eia2024bess,nrel2021bess,
nrel2024bess}. The same coefficients are used for both half-year rounds unless
the instructor announces a revised round file. The reference literature also
reports fixed O\&M near \USD40/kW-year; the current evaluator does not have a
separate annual fixed-O\&M field, so that value is documented as a reference
and is not charged in addition to the \USD8/MWh operating proxy.

\subsection{DCOPF}

For each hour, the instructor solves a linear \code{DCOPF} on the prepared Texas
network. Network balance, generator limits, branch thermal limits, and fixed
base demand are enforced. Load is inelastic in the ordinary feasible case:
there is no integer unit commitment and no ordinary load-shedding variable.

\section{CSV submission format}\label{sec:csv}

Submit one \code{UTF-8 CSV} file per team. The canonical header is:

\begin{lstlisting}
team_id,submission_id,period,bus_id,load_mw,generator_id,generator_capacity_mw,storage_id,storage_power_mw,storage_energy_mwh
\end{lstlisting}

Each row contains a one-based hour \code{period} and an ACTIVSg2000 \code{bus\_id}. A \code{generator}
or \code{storage} specification may be repeated on rows for the same team; its
capacity and attachment must remain consistent. The load for a given
bus--period must also remain consistent across repeated resource rows.

For a released half-year submission, each selected bus must cover every period
at least once; additional rows may declare distinct generator or storage
resources, provided the load and resource specifications remain consistent. The short table below illustrates
the column format only; it is not a complete half-year submission. A file that
contains only the illustrated rows will be rejected when checked against a
released year and round.

Example:
\begin{lstlisting}
team_id,submission_id,period,bus_id,load_mw,generator_id,generator_capacity_mw,storage_id,storage_power_mw,storage_energy_mwh
team-blue,half_year_1,1,3002041,40,12875,20,BATT_1,10,20
team-blue,half_year_1,2,3002041,35,12875,20,BATT_1,10,20
team-blue,half_year_1,1,3002042,25,,,,,
\end{lstlisting}

Use empty fields for resources that are not present on a row. Values use MW,
MWh, and decimal notation. Periods are one-based in the CSV even though the
internal evaluator uses zero-based indices.

The \code{validator} also checks complete horizon coverage. This prevents a short
pilot file from silently becoming a mostly-zero half-year plan. The released
checker accepts the horizon implied by the selected year and round.

\textbf{Before submitting, run the provided \code{CSV validator}.} From the project
root, run with the released ACTIVSg2000 scenario-block path:
\begin{lstlisting}
PYTHONPATH=src python scripts/check_submission.py \\
  --file team-blue.csv \\
  --artifact-dir data/processed/activsg2000_scenarios_v1/2017/jan_jun \\
  --year 2017 --round jan\_jun
\end{lstlisting}
Use \code{--year 2017 --round jul\_dec} for the July--December submission. The \code{checker} is
also available after installation as \code{ercot-game-check-csv}. It checks UTF-8
encoding, the exact header, numeric fields, bus and generator IDs, duplicate
keys, consistent resource specifications, horizon bounds, and nonnegative
values. The validator is a convenience check; the instructor evaluator remains
authoritative.

\subsection{Complete small example}
The following illustrates a valid two-hour pattern, but it is not a valid
course submission because it is shorter than either released horizon:
\begin{lstlisting}
team_id,submission_id,period,bus_id,load_mw,generator_id,generator_capacity_mw,storage_id,storage_power_mw,storage_energy_mwh
team-demo,demo-1,1,3002041,4,12875,2,BATT_1,1,2
team-demo,demo-1,2,3002041,3,12875,2,BATT_1,1,2
team-demo,demo-1,1,3002042,2,,,,,
team-demo,demo-1,2,3002042,0,,,,,
\end{lstlisting}
The resource specifications are repeated consistently for the first bus. The
second bus has no generator or storage, so those fields are empty. In an actual
submission, this coverage pattern continues through period 4,368 or 4,416.

\subsection{Checker troubleshooting}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Unknown bus or generator:} copy the ID from the supplied
  ACTIVSg2000 catalog; do not invent a new ID.
  \item \textbf{Incomplete horizon:} add rows for every missing period on each
  selected bus, using zero load when the bus is idle.
  \item \textbf{Inconsistent resource specification:} repeat the same
  capacity and attachment values on every row for that resource.
  \item \textbf{Malformed CSV:} save as UTF-8 CSV and inspect commas,
  quotation marks, and the exact header.
\end{itemize}

\section{How joint evaluation works}

The instructor first rejects \code{malformed CSV} files in isolation; a malformed file
does not prevent other teams from being evaluated. Review the exact file
contract in Section~\ref{sec:csv} before diagnosing a rejection.

Valid plans are then evaluated jointly. If the joint plan is \code{feasible}, each
team is scored using its cleared hourly load, private resource dispatch, and
the announced scoring vectors.

If the joint plan is \code{infeasible}, the evaluator applies the \code{fair
continuous projection}, discussed in Section~\ref{sec:projection}. No first-come-first-serve ordering is used. The projected
load is then used for the score. The capital costs associated with submitted
sites, generators, and storage remain part of the score. See Section~\ref{sec:score} for the score calculation.

\section{Fair continuous projection}\label{sec:projection}

Let $d_{i,s,t}$ be the requested load of team $i$ at bus $s$ at time $t$. When the full joint request is
infeasible, the evaluator finds a common continuous service fraction
$\alpha\in[0,1]$ and evaluates the scaled requests
\[
  \widehat d_{i,s,t}=\alpha d_{i,s,t}.
\]
The largest feasible $\alpha$ is selected to \emph{maximize the minimum fraction of
each team's requested plan that is served.} This policy has three intended properties:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item every valid team remains in the competition;
  \item teams are treated symmetrically at the service-fraction level;
  \item the projected dispatch remains a physically feasible DCOPF solution.
\end{enumerate}

The policy is related to \code{max--min} and proportional fairness ideas used in
resource allocation and demand-response research~\cite{kelly1998charging,
jacubowicz2022fairness,gebbran2021fairder}. It is a teaching rule, not an
ERCOT operating procedure.

\section{Scoring}\label{sec:score}

For team $i$, the \code{score} is
\[
\begin{aligned}
S_i={}V_i^{\mathrm{IT}}-C_i^{\mathrm{energy}}
-C_i^{\mathrm{gen,op}}-C_i^{\mathrm{gen,cap}}-C_i^{\mathrm{storage,op}}-C_i^{\mathrm{storage,cap}}
-C_i^{\mathrm{site}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The components are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item $V_i^{\mathrm{IT}}$: cleared load multiplied by the full-horizon hourly
  \code{IT value} vector announced for the round. The current release uses
  \USD125/MWh at every hour;
  \item $C_i^{\mathrm{energy}}$: grid purchase multiplied by the locational
  hourly \code{energy price} artifact for the selected bus. The final artifact
  is generated from baseline ACTIVSg2000 DCOPF LMPs;
  \item $C_i^{\mathrm{gen,op}}$: private-generator dispatch multiplied by the
  dataset-derived marginal cost;
  \item $C_i^{\mathrm{gen,cap}}$: submitted generator MW multiplied by the
  announced generator capital coefficient;
  \item $C_i^{\mathrm{storage,op}}$: announced \code{storage operating coefficient}
  multiplied by storage throughput;
  \item $C_i^{\mathrm{storage,cap}}$: announced storage MW/MWh capital
  coefficients multiplied by submitted capacities;
  \item $C_i^{\mathrm{site}}$: number of submitted \code{connection sites} multiplied
  by the announced site coefficient.
\end{itemize}

The current provisional calibration anchors are listed below. The instructor
must distribute the final hourly vectors and storage coefficients with the
round configuration; do not infer them from this manual if the round file
differs.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{0.94\textwidth}{>{\ttfamily}l r X}
\toprule
Parameter & Provisional value & Basis\\
\midrule
Generator capital & \USD85,000/MW & Annualized combustion-turbine anchor from EIA cost data~\cite{eia2024generatorcosts}.\\
Site cost & \USD50,000/site & Instructional interconnection proxy informed by Berkeley Lab interconnection-cost work~\cite{lbnl2022interconnection}.\\
Generator operating cost & Dataset value & ACTIVSg2000 \code{gencost} C1 record.\\
Energy price & Baseline LMP artifact & Exogenous ACTIVSg2000 DCOPF price surface, one price per bus and hour.\\
IT value & \USD125/MWh hourly & Full-horizon instructor-chosen opportunity value; see the profile documentation.\\
Storage power capital & \USD200,000/MW & Rounded power-capacity component calibrated from NREL/EIA battery benchmarks~\cite{eia2024bess,nrel2024bess}.\\
Storage energy capital & \USD300,000/MWh & Rounded energy-capacity component; together with power capital gives about \USD1,400/kW for a four-hour system.\\
Storage operation & \USD8/MWh discharged & Variable O\&M proxy; the reference fixed O\&M value is \USD40/kW-year~\cite{eia2024bess}.\\
Invalid file score & 0 & Deterministic grading policy, not a market quantity.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\subsection{How to reason about your score}
The evaluator first determines how much of your requested load is served. It
then adds IT value for served load and subtracts energy, site, generator, and
storage costs. A larger plan is therefore not automatically better: an
additional site may increase IT value while also increasing energy,
congestion, and capital costs. A generator or storage device can reduce grid
purchases but still lower the final score if its costs exceed the avoided
energy cost.

Use the full-horizon \code{IT-value} vector and the locational \code{price}
artifact supplied with the round when estimating a score. The current IT-value
vector is constant by policy; the energy price is not a student-chosen value.

\section{Practical workflow}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Read the round configuration and identify the horizon.
  \item Inspect the ACTIVSg2000 bus and generator tables supplied by the instructor.
  \item Choose one or more buses and build hourly load profiles.
  \item Decide whether private generation or storage improves your score.
  \item Generate the \code{CSV} and run the \code{validator}.
  \item Keep the exact submitted file and validator output for reproducibility.
  \item Submit before the announced deadline.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What to inspect after evaluation}
Read the result in this order:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item feasibility or projected service status;
  \item total score and rank;
  \item cleared load, grid purchases, and resource costs;
  \item binding or limiting network diagnostics;
  \item storage state and generator dispatch;
  \item raw audit output only when debugging.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Final submission checklist}
Before uploading, confirm:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item the file is \code{UTF-8 CSV} with the exact header;
  \item every selected bus has one row for every required period;
  \item all bus and generator IDs come from the supplied catalogs;
  \item generator and storage specifications are consistent across rows;
  \item all loads and capacities are finite and nonnegative;
  \item the \code{checker} passes with the correct half-year horizon;
  \item you retained the exact submitted file and checker output;
  \item your strategy note explains the main economic and physical trade-offs.
\end{itemize}

\section{Troubleshooting and frequently asked questions}
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=0pt]
  \item[Can I invent a generator ID or choose its operating cost?]
  No. \code{Generator IDs} and operating costs come from the supplied ACTIVSg2000 catalog.
  You choose capacity; the evaluator dispatches the generator.

  \item[Can my generator or storage serve another team?]
  No. These resources are private and can reduce only your team's grid
  purchase.

  \item[What happens if the joint plan is infeasible?]
  The evaluator first applies the fair continuous projection in
  Section~\ref{sec:projection}, using one common service fraction for valid
  teams.

  \item[Why is my score negative?]
  Served-load value may be smaller than energy, site, generator, or storage
  costs. Inspect the detailed score components before changing the plan.

  \item[Which result is official?]
  The instructor-generated result and audit files determine the official
  score. Local calculations are for debugging and strategy development.
\end{description}
